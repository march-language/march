# Build stability tooling before more logic

The biggest lesson from fifteen years of Dafny, Verus, F*, Liquid Haskell and SPARK is that a verifier lives or dies on engineering, not expressiveness. That means predictable proofs, small solver contexts, explanations of failures, and specs that can be executed. Most of March's current roadmap adds logic: unbounded quantified lemmas, Lean export, checked solver proofs, proof scripts. The evidence says the next investment should go to tooling that matures tools already have and March lacks. That tooling is deterministic resource budgets, relevance-pruned contexts, a stability (brittleness) measurement mode gated in CI, isolated sub-queries for bit-vector and nonlinear arithmetic, proof-dependency diagnostics (vacuity and unused hypotheses), and boundary type invariants. **About 2–5% of queries in large Dafny and F* projects are unstable. Irrelevant context explains 78% of the instability, and a single bad quantifier explains 61% of unstable queries** ([Mariposa](https://www.andrew.cmu.edu/user/bparno/papers/mariposa.pdf), [SHAKE](https://www.andrew.cmu.edu/user/bparno/papers/shake.pdf), [Cazamariposas](https://www.andrew.cmu.edu/user/bparno/papers/cazamariposas.pdf)). That makes unbounded triggered lemmas the riskiest roadmap item unless this infrastructure lands first. In production, AWS reports that brittleness delayed deployments of its Dafny authorization engine by up to 14 days, until CI variance metrics and opacity refactoring cut its brittle VCs from 41 to 4 ([AuthV2](https://cdn.amazon.science/bb/40/22ac44f84f6d8eb625ac9666a00f/formally-verified-cloud-scale-authorization.pdf)). March's actor model hands it IronFleet's hardest-won simplification for free: a handler is an atomic step. Its hot-reload migration story fills a real gap in the literature. On trust, the notes contain no study blaming false proofs on solver bugs, while every documented assurance failure sits in specs, encodings or harnesses. Checked solver proofs and Lean export therefore address the smallest part of the trusted base and should wait behind cheaper encoding validation and vacuity checks.

## Context and quantifiers, not solver bugs, make proofs brittle

The instability research converges on a clear causal picture. Mariposa ran 17,043 SMT queries from six Dafny and F* projects through semantics-preserving mutations: assertion shuffling, alpha-renaming and reseeding. **Z3 4.12.1 was unstable on 2.6% of queries overall and up to 5.0% for individual projects.** Solver upgrades made Dafny projects *less* stable: a single Z3 commit that reordered AST terms caused 40% of the regressions between 4.8.5 and 4.8.8 ([Mariposa](https://www.andrew.cmu.edu/user/bparno/papers/mariposa.pdf)). Longer timeouts are no fix: raising the limit from 60 s to 150 s left the unstable share "remarkably consistent." The follow-up work locates the cause. Typically **96–99% of assertions in a query are absent from the unsat core**, and pruning to the core cures 78% of unstable instances ([SHAKE](https://www.andrew.cmu.edu/user/bparno/papers/shake.pdf)). In 61% of 615 unstable queries across 12 Dafny, F* and Verus projects, one quantified formula was to blame, and automated repair fixed about 70% of them, 87% by touching a single quantifier ([Cazamariposas](https://www.andrew.cmu.edu/user/bparno/papers/cazamariposas.pdf)). The counter-view deserves a fair hearing. An EPFL/MSR deep dive into 11 unstable queries found fixable root causes in all of them: six were Z3 bugs, two were misconfigurations and three were trigger misunderstandings. Each took several person-days to diagnose ([Conjecture paper](https://dslab.epfl.ch/pubs/smt-instability-conjecture.pdf)). Both views lead to the same engineering conclusion for March: keep the context small, keep quantifiers few and well-triggered, and pin the solver.

Two data points speak directly to March's design. First, decidable fragments are markedly more stable. KomodoS, written in a fragment with statically bounded recursion, was 0.52% unstable, against 4.27% for KomodoD restricted to the same features. vWasm in F* was nearly fully stable because its authors tuned fuel, minimized ambient lemmas and quantifiers, and hand-picked triggers ([Mariposa](https://www.andrew.cmu.edu/user/bparno/papers/mariposa.pdf)). March's quantifier-free refinements and bounded quantifiers already sit in this favourable regime. Its reflected definitions and measures, if encoded as universally quantified axioms, are exactly the "ambient axiom" category the research blames. Second, failures are bimodal. In Verus projects about 43% of failures arrive in under a second and the rest hit the limit, with almost nothing in between. Quick unknowns come from *missing* instantiations, and timeouts from *excessive* ones: median instantiation counts are 270,396 for timeouts against 4,587 for quick unknowns ([Cazamariposas](https://www.andrew.cmu.edu/user/bparno/papers/cazamariposas.pdf)). March can use this as a free classifier on every failure. A fast unknown means "add a lemma call, reveal or fuel"; a resource-limit hit means "fix a trigger or make something opaque."

Mature tools have converged on the same levers, and March has only some of them. **Verus aggressively prunes unreachable definitions from each query**, defaults recursive fuel to 1, hides opaque and closed bodies, and isolates nonlinear and bit-vector reasoning into context-free sub-queries. Its IronKV port produced **95% smaller queries and 10x faster verification** than the Dafny original: 41 s against 445 s on one core ([Verus SOSP 2024](https://www.andrew.cmu.edu/user/bparno/papers/verus-sys.pdf)). Verus, Dafny and AWS all budget solver effort in Z3's deterministic **rlimit** rather than wall-clock time, so a limit is independent of machine speed ([Verus guide](https://verus-lang.github.io/verus/guide/print.html)). Dafny ships the most mature brittleness measurement. `measure-complexity --iterations N` varies seed, order and names, and the report generator bounds the coefficient of variation of resource count, with a recommended target under 20% "perhaps even as low as 5%" ([Dafny Reference Manual](https://dafny.org/latest/DafnyRef/DafnyRef)). F* records unsat cores as hint files and replays only those facts in a fresh solver, falling back to the full context when replay fails ([F* tutorial](https://fstar-lang.org/tutorial/book/under_the_hood/uth_smt.html)). Liquid Haskell takes a different route. Proof by Logical Evaluation instantiates reflected definitions only by guided symbolic evaluation, never as free axioms. It is complete for equational proofs over total functions, and its higher-order extension PLEX costs only 5% on LH's test suite ([Refinement Reflection](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1711.03842), [PLEX](https://webspace.science.uu.nl/~swier004/publications/2026-oopsla.pdf)).

On triggers, Verus deliberately chooses "as few triggers as possible" and prints a note when its automatic choice has low confidence, accepting more initial effort for better scalability. It also ships a built-in quantifier profiler that launches automatically on timeout and attributes cost to the culprit quantifier rather than "innocent bystanders" ([Verus SOSP 2024](https://www.andrew.cmu.edu/user/bparno/papers/verus-sys.pdf), [Verus guide](https://verus-lang.github.io/verus/guide/print.html)). Dafny moved trigger selection out of Z3 and warns on suspected matching loops ([Leino & Pit-Claudel](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/trigger-selection-strategies-stabilize-program-verifiers/)). Multi-solver portfolios work in the Why3 family: SPARK tries cvc5, Z3, colibri and Alt-Ergo in turn. Z3-centric tools cannot follow, because their queries are "overfitted to Z3": cvc5 solved only about 14% of converted KomodoD queries ([Mariposa](https://www.andrew.cmu.edu/user/bparno/papers/mariposa.pdf), [GNATprove](https://docs.adacore.com/spark2014-docs/html/ug/en/appendix/command_line_invocation.html)). Pruned queries help cvc5 more than Z3, with a 41% stability gain against 29% ([SHAKE](https://www.andrew.cmu.edu/user/bparno/papers/shake.pdf)). For March, the cheap insurance is to emit only standard SMT-LIB with standard `:pattern` syntax now, so a cvc5 fallback stays possible later.

## Users want to see what the verifier knows, not just that it failed

Four recent studies agree that proof debugging, not code writing, is the dominant pain. Fourteen experienced Dafny users described error messages as "a black box" that "spits out a no". One asked how to know "which of the 1,000 things involved in that assert" was the problem. The authors name the core difficulty as building a mental model of **implicit verifier state**, which interactive provers display and auto-active tools hide ([Mugnier et al. OOPSLA 2025](https://cseweb.ucsd.edu/~mcoblenz/assets/pdf/OOPSLA_2025_Dafny.pdf)). Every participant debugged by sprinkling intermediate assertions or by `assume`-ing a suspected fact. Brittleness was "unanimously acknowledged as a major issue," and one participant wished opaque were the default. The Liquid Haskell study, the closest analogue to March, found barriers that are almost all engineering. Parser nonterminals leaked into messages, ill-formed predicates were reported as verification failures, highlights landed at the alias use site instead of its definition, and internal names such as `VV##0` appeared in output. Experienced users worked around the opacity by **executing functions to test edge cases** ([Gamboa et al. PLDI 2025](https://catarinagamboa.github.io/papers/pre_print_barriers_liquid_types.pdf)). An ETH survey of 29 practitioners across 27 tools found 16 complaining of slow verifiers and 12 of proof maintenance. Participants could not tell whether a failure came from the tool, the solver or the program ([arXiv 2510.20514](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.20514v2)). Telemetry from F* and Verus experts showed the same universal workaround of commenting out code or inserting `assume(false)` to isolate one sub-goal. The authors recommend **breakpoint-style toggles to ignore lines** ([arXiv 2508.02733](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.02733v1)).

March already answers the "what" well, through per-conjunct messages, an internals-name lint and executed counterexamples. Its gaps are the "why" and the context. Dafny's unsat-core proof-dependency analysis is the most advanced shipped answer. It reports **vacuous proofs** (the goal was never used, so assumptions are contradictory), **redundant assumptions**, and code no proof constrains, at about 30% extra verification time ([Dafny blog](https://dafny.org/blog/2023/10/27/proof-dependencies/)). For refinement types the three outputs map directly onto a vacuity warning, an unused-hypothesis hint and a "facts used" hover on a discharged obligation. That hover is the closest auto-active substitute for the goal view users asked for. Unsat cores say nothing about *failed* goals. There, the matching feature is a source-syntax dump of the facts in scope at the failure point: path conditions, parameter refinements and `assert`/`by` hypotheses. No surveyed tool ships this, and users rebuild it by hand with dozens of assertions. Verus's `--expand-errors` pushes a failed goal *through* spec-function definitions to the failing inner conjunct, the natural extension of March's conjunct splitting ([Verus checklist](https://verus-lang.github.io/verus/guide/checklist.html)).

Counterexamples are where March is already ahead. Only one of 14 Dafny experts used counterexamples, yet 10 of 29 ETH participants valued them. That gap suggests raw SMT models are rarely usable, and Viper's retrospective admits its counterexamples must be stitched from two different sources ([Eilers et al. 2025](https://pm.inf.ethz.ch/publications/EilersSchwerhoffSummersMueller25.pdf)). Because March re-runs the model in its interpreter, values are always source-level. The remaining opportunity is a label no tool offers. A counterexample is either **"confirmed by execution"**, meaning a real bug, or **"model did not reproduce"**, meaning incompleteness: a missing lemma, nonlinear arithmetic or a lost instantiation. That label directly answers the ETH finding that users cannot tell program failures from tool failures.

LLM agents are a new class of user, and the evidence carries a sharp warning. AutoVerus reported 91.3% on its benchmark. An independent reproduction got 75.3% on the original Verus build and **52.7% on a 2025 Verus build**, because its prompts were keyed to old error text ([AutoVerus](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2409.13082), [ExVerus](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.25810)). For agents, diagnostics are an API. March's `--check-json` and diagnostic slugs should be versioned and should carry the obligation id, the failing conjunct, the executed counterexample and the in-scope facts. The same structure that helps humans feeds the better pipelines, which use error localization and counterexamples ([Laurel](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2405.16792)). Agents also produce verifier-accepted but weak specs, which makes vacuity and spec testing more important, not less ([VerusEval](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.26464)).

## Production verification is one-third proof and two-thirds everything else

AWS's AuthV2 engine handles over a billion calls per second and was rewritten in Dafny over four years. Its authors split the effort **evenly between spec, proofs and implementation; compilation; and testing and validation** ([AuthV2](https://cdn.amazon.science/bb/40/22ac44f84f6d8eb625ac9666a00f/formally-verified-cloud-scale-authorization.pdf)). The spec was written to be executable. Differential testing against the old engine found 13 spec mismatches, and shadow testing of 10^15 production samples found corner cases the spec had not modelled. Brittleness was the main operational cost: "most code or environment changes broke at least one proof". The team fixed it with three measures: a uniform cloud verification environment, a CI gate on the coefficient of variation of resource counts that blocks PRs which degrade stability, and refactoring "mostly [by] making functions opaque." Resource counts fell by two orders of magnitude on the brittle parts. **The price was more proof text**: one module's proof grew from 300 to 1,100 lines, and the team judged predictability worth it. Cedar shows the same pattern with Lean. An executable Lean model is differential-tested against Rust, and **testing found 21 bugs against 4 found by proofs**. Releases are gated on the model, proofs and tests being current ([Cedar VGD](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.01688v1), [AWS blog](https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/opensource/lean-into-verified-software-development)).

Microsoft's Everest retrospective sharpens the picture. Its durable outputs were not a verified TLS stack, whose handshake proof was "left incomplete". They were verified components emitted as readable C: HACL★ in Firefox, Linux and Python, and EverParse in Windows, which has run five years "without incident" and whose C "has never been patched directly" ([Everest retrospective](https://project-everest.github.io/assets/everest-perspectives-2025.pdf)). Its authors credit components over monoliths, performance parity and readable output, and they wish for tools that automatically wrap verified APIs with **defensive runtime-checked variants** for unverified callers. Their most relevant warning for March is that migrating Low★ code to the new Steel framework was "too labor intensive" and was abandoned, so changing the language under existing proofs stranded them. They also write that minimizing the trusted computing base "is not always the leading factor in enabling adoption."

SPARK is the longest-running industrial deployment, and its adoption model is graded. Stone is a valid subset, Bronze guarantees data flow, Silver guarantees absence of run-time errors, Gold proves key properties, and Platinum proves full functional correctness. Teams bank cheap guarantees across the whole codebase and reserve functional proof for a few modules ([EE Journal](https://www.eejournal.com/industry_news/new-spark-adoption-guidance-published/)). NVIDIA's security team adopted SPARK because assurance "was not measurable" in C ([AdaCore](https://adacore.com/papers/nvidia-adoption-of-spark-new-era-in-security-critical-software-development)). Liquid Haskell, the design closest to March, has no documented industrial case study. Its maintainers say it grew "mostly from academic contributions," and reflection remains "experimental" for usability and performance reasons ([Tweag](https://tweag.io/blog/2025-02-06-refactoring-lh/), [Tweag release](https://tweag.io/blog/2025-03-20-lh-release/)). The lesson for March is that its strict `cap verified` gate is a Platinum-only switch. A per-module, compiler-reported assurance level would let a codebase adopt verification incrementally, and Silver corresponds to what March's division-safety and precondition checks already discharge.

## March's actor model already pays IronFleet's hardest bill

IronFleet verified Paxos and a sharded key-value store. Its central simplification was a reduction obligation: each host step performs all receives before all sends, with at most one time-dependent operation. That lets real interleavings be reordered into atomic steps, so per-host code can be verified with sequential Hoare logic. The work cost about 3.7 person-years at a 3.6:1 proof-to-code ratio, and the obligation itself was justified only informally ([IronFleet](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2015/10/ironfleet.pdf)). A March actor handler that runs one message to completion against private state *is* that atomic step. March's per-handler invariants are IronFleet's implementation layer. The guarantee holds only under conditions March should state explicitly in its verification semantics. A synchronous `call` that blocks mid-handler breaks atomicity and needs a split-step model. Clock reads and receive-timeouts must be modelled as nondeterministic inputs. Message ordering (per-pair FIFO or not) decides which protocol invariants are provable at all.

The missing piece is a protocol layer: an abstract transition system over all actors plus in-flight messages, with per-handler checks discharging "this handler implements transition T." Verus's VerusSync shows how to make this usable. Users write Ivy-style transitions with per-field sharding strategies. The tool generates linear ghost tokens and inductiveness obligations, and users never see the underlying resource algebra. The Node Replication port needed far fewer proof lines and verified two orders of magnitude faster than its Linear Dafny predecessor ([Verus SOSP 2024](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10630619)). March already has linear types and ghost erasure, so ghost linear tokens cost almost nothing to add. For example, a token for "I am leader for epoch e" can travel inside a session-typed message, and linearity alone guarantees uniqueness. Industry practice suggests a cheaper tier beneath the deductive one. AWS models S3's strong-consistency protocols in P and model-checks executable state machines ([CACM/ACM Queue](https://queue.acm.org/detail.cfm?id=3712057)). DuoAI infers inductive invariants automatically for 27 textbook protocols, solving Paxos more than two orders of magnitude faster than prior tools ([DuoAI](https://www.usenix.org/conference/osdi22/presentation/yao)). March's interpreter can drive both: schedule exploration with `old(state)` handler specs as assertions, and logged reachable states as invariant candidates. The closest prior art to March's session-plus-refinement design is refined multiparty session types (Session*). They put SMT-checked refinements on message payloads with little runtime overhead ([Zhou et al. OOPSLA 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2009.06541)). The natural March extension relates payloads to the sender's or receiver's state.

For effects, the evidence favours two cheap mechanisms over heavy logic. SPARK's `Global` and `Depends` contracts state which state a subprogram touches and which inputs influence which outputs, and they are **checked by flow analysis, not proof** ([learn.adacore.com](https://learn.adacore.com/courses/intro-to-spark/chapters/02_Flow_Analysis.html)). That is the right shape for March's capability effect specs. IronFleet specifies I/O as a ghost event journal (`journal == old(journal) ++ ios`). This gives two-state effect specs a ready template: a per-handler `ensures sends == ...` over a ghost trace. F*'s rule that ghost (GTot) computation never flows into total code is the soundness condition March's erasure must enforce: a ghost value must never decide a runtime branch ([F* book](https://fstar-lang.org/tutorial/book/part4/part4_ghost.html)). Prusti's lesson is to derive frame facts from the type system rather than ask for permission annotations. March's unaliased actor state makes the `old(state)` frame trivial. Shared-memory concurrency, the expensive part of Verus, Armada and Iris, is largely absent, which is why Atmosphere still uses a big lock ([Atmosphere](https://mars-research.github.io/doc/2025-sosp-atmo.pdf)).

Hot code reload is March's chance to lead. The main verified dynamic-update work, from around 2012, analysed a merged old-and-new program, but **left the hand-written state transformer unverified** ([Hayden et al.](https://www.cs.umd.edu/~mwh/papers/hayden12dsucorrect.html)). A study of 123 real upgrade failures found about two-thirds came from versions disagreeing on data format, 60% of those in persistent state and 40% in network messages ([Zhang et al. SOSP 2021](https://www.cs.purdue.edu/homes/yonglezh/pub/upgrade-sosp21.pdf)). Fuzzing still finds new ones in Cassandra and HDFS ([UpFuzz NSDI 2026](https://www.usenix.org/conference/nsdi26/presentation/han)). March can make `old_inv(s) ==> new_inv(migrate(s))` a single SMT obligation, because both invariants are already per-actor refinements. It can check protocol compatibility between versions as session subtyping in both directions, and add an explicit obligation that the new handler accept old-format messages still in the mailbox. No auto-active tool the research found does this. Liveness can wait. IronFleet and Anvil show it is achievable with fairness axioms, ranking functions and an explicit fault model, but it is the most expensive frontier, and Grove and Armada stop at safety ([Anvil](https://www.usenix.org/conference/osdi24/presentation/sun-xudong)).

## Specification gaps cluster at abstraction boundaries and collections

The single most useful missing feature for proving March's HAMT Map/Set is an **abstraction-boundary invariant**. SPARK's `Type_Invariant` holds only outside a private type's package. It is assumed on entry to visible subprograms and checked on return, and helpers may break it temporarily ([SPARK UG](https://docs.adacore.com/spark2014-docs/html/ug/en/source/type_contracts.html)). Verus's `type_invariant` adds an obligation at every constructor and is *not* injected automatically: code calls `use_type_invariant`, a deliberate choice to keep contexts small ([Verus guide](https://verus-lang.github.io/verus/guide/reference-type-invariants.html)). March's refinement aliases are always-true value predicates, the other design, and cannot express "well-formed except inside my own helpers." Next comes the visibility split every mature tool makes between a declaration and its body. Dafny has `provides` versus `reveals`, Verus has `pub open` versus `pub closed`, and F* has `.fsti` interfaces. Verus states explicitly that `opaque` is for performance and `closed` is for modularity ([Verus guide](https://verus-lang.github.io/verus/guide/opaque.html), [Dafny Reference Manual](https://dafny.org/latest/DafnyRef/DafnyRef)). March has only the performance knob. Once unbounded lemmas arrive, Verus's opt-in **broadcast groups** matter too. Its guide warns that "every extra ambient fact slows the prover" ([Verus broadcast](https://verus-lang.github.io/verus/guide/broadcast_proof.html)).

HAMT proofs also need theories March lacks. Verus isolates `by(bit_vector)` and `by(nonlinear_arith)` into separate queries with no ambient context. Only facts passed through an explicit `requires` reach them, which keeps the default context linear and fast ([Verus bit vectors](https://verus-lang.github.io/verus/guide/bitvec.html), [Verus nonlinear](https://verus-lang.github.io/verus/guide/nonlinear.html)). Popcount-and-mask indexing is the textbook case for it. Because compiled March Ints are 63-bit, such a mode must model that width or bridge to 64 bits with an explicit truncation lemma. Collections need **extensional equality** as an explicit operator, Verus's `=~=`. That emits the extensionality lemma only on demand, and Verus documents its collection axioms as deliberately "incomplete" ([Verus TOC](https://verus-lang.github.io/verus/guide/toc.html)). Permutation properties such as "sort returns the same elements" need a multiset theory, which set-valued measures cannot express ([Dafny Reference Manual](https://dafny.org/latest/DafnyRef/DafnyRef)).

On the proof side, `assert forall x | P(x) implies Q(x) by { ... }` lets an unbounded lemma be *proved* by an arbitrary instance rather than only stated. It belongs in the same release as triggered lemmas. Dafny's `by method` pairs a clean recursive spec function with an efficient implementation proved equal once. This suits a Perceus-optimized stdlib. Verus's `by(compute_only)` and LH's PLE offer proof by evaluation as an alternative to manual fuel ([Verus compute](https://verus-lang.github.io/verus/guide/assert_by_compute.html)). Dafny `twostate` lemmas let users state reusable facts across `old` and new state, not only ensures clauses. Greatest predicates with finite prefix approximations are the established route to coinductive stream properties of actors. Finally, the ecosystem point: Dafny ships pre-verified standard lemma libraries that users never re-verify, and that move is what made reuse happen ([Dafny standard libraries](https://github.com/dafny-lang/dafny/blob/master/Source/DafnyStandardLibraries/README.md)). Executable specs close the loop. Dafny's `generate-tests` turns verifier machinery into coverage-directed unit tests ([DTest](https://assets.amazon.science/b9/d4/eeb4fe1a47609f75956dd6fbc0c6/a-toolkit-for-automated-testing-of-dafny.pdf)). March can go further by running refinements and postconditions as `Check.all` oracles.

## The trusted base that fails in practice is the encoding, not z3

The research gathered here contains **no study attributing a false "verified" result to a z3 soundness bug**. The solver bugs that do appear, six of eleven in the EPFL study, caused *instability*: timeouts and unknowns, failures in the safe direction ([Conjecture paper](https://dslab.epfl.ch/pubs/smt-instability-conjecture.pdf)). The notes contain no quantified study of where false proofs come from, so the claim cannot be pinned to a number here. Every documented assurance failure, however, sits above the solver. AuthV2's specification missed corner cases that only shadow testing exposed, and the project carries 1,557 lines of trusted library axiomatization ([AuthV2](https://cdn.amazon.science/bb/40/22ac44f84f6d8eb625ac9666a00f/formally-verified-cloud-scale-authorization.pdf)). Cedar's tests found five times more bugs than its proofs. The Rust standard library effort labels some verified results "a triage signal, not a complete safety proof," because they rest on unconstrained inputs ([Rust Foundation](https://rustfoundation.org/media/how-the-rust-standard-library-verification-contest-scaled-past-manual-proof-engineering/)). Soundness research on translational verifiers concentrates on the source-to-IVL translation layers ([Parthasarathy et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.03614v2), [arXiv 2603.20001](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.20001)), and Dafny's own wiki recommends negating a postcondition to catch vacuous proofs ([Cautionary Tales](https://github.com/dafny-lang/dafny/wiki/Cautionary-Tales)).

For March, then, the risky components are its own. They are the VC generator, the encoding of 63-bit Int, the measure and reflection axioms, fuel unfolding, ghost erasure, and the VC cache key. Checked solver proofs (Alethe, LFSC or similar) would certify only the final step, from SMT query to unsat, and say nothing about whether the query faithfully encodes the March program. Lean export certifies more if the full semantics is modelled, but it is a multi-person-year undertaking. The notes found no shipped "Lean as backend" tool to copy, and Cedar's Lean experience still cost slow mutual-induction termination checking and custom containers. March owns an asset that SMT-only tools lack: an interpreter that defines its semantics. Differential testing of the encoding against that interpreter attacks the actual weak point at a fraction of the cost. Generate inputs, check that VCs the solver calls valid are never falsified by execution, and flag counterexamples the interpreter refuses to reproduce. Add vacuity warnings, a negated-postcondition smoke test, an `--audit` report of every trusted assumption, and pinning of the z3 version in the cache key. March's existing refine oracle already guards encoding stability across compiler versions, the Low★-to-Steel lesson.

## Prioritized recommendations

Evidence strength is rated **Strong** when backed by measured results from multiple projects or tools, **Moderate** when backed by one measured project or consistent qualitative studies, and **Weak** when backed only by design claims or analogy. Effort is a rough estimate for a compiler team already familiar with March's refinement checker: S is days, M is a few weeks, L is one to three months, and XL is more than a quarter.

| # | Adopt | Why | Evidence | Effort |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Deterministic z3 rlimit budgets per VC (with per-function override), rlimit usage recorded in the VC cache, z3 version and options in the cache key | Machine-independent limits; cost jumps become an early warning without extra solver runs; solver upgrades flip hundreds of queries | Strong (AuthV2, Verus, Dafny, Mariposa) | S |

Several roadmap items should be resequenced or deprioritized. **Unbounded quantified lemmas with triggers** are the highest-risk item, because quantifiers are the dominant instability source. Ship them only after items 1–3 and 5, with conservative trigger selection, low-confidence notes, static matching-loop detection and opt-in broadcast groups. **Checked solver proofs** should drop well down the list: they certify a component with no documented false-proof record, while the encoding and specs, where failures do occur, stay unchecked. Do items 7 and 6 first, and revisit only if a certification customer demands a small trusted base. **Lean export** carries XL cost against evidence that TCB minimization rarely drives adoption, and the notes found no shipped Lean-backend tool to learn from. Treat it as research, not roadmap. **Proof scripts** have the weakest support. Experts in the studies ask for focus/skip toggles and visibility, and Verus and Dafny scale with `assert … by` structure plus by-modes rather than tactics. vWasm's use of F* tactics for controlled normalization is the one counterexample, and items 4 and 14 cover most of that need. **Two-state `old(state)` actor specs**, **capability effect specs** and **proving the HAMT contracts** remain well-supported. Implement effect specs as a SPARK-style dataflow check plus a ghost journal rather than as SMT. Make the HAMT proof depend on items 4 and 8.

## Conclusion

The research reframes what "more verification" means for March. The successful auto-active tools did not win by adding logic; they won by making the logic they had predictable, explainable and testable. Every one of them retrofitted brittleness tooling at real cost: Dafny's report generator, Verus's pruning and rlimits, F*'s hints, AWS's CI gate. March can build it in before it has a large proof corpus to strand, the step from which Everest never recovered with Steel. Its interpreter-executed semantics is an underrated asset. It makes counterexamples trustworthy, spec testing cheap and encoding validation possible, which addresses the parts of the trusted base that fail in practice better than certifying the solver.

The second shift is that March's distinctive features (actors with atomic handlers, linear types, session types and hot reload) line up with the hardest lessons of IronFleet, VerusSync and the upgrade-failure literature better than any surveyed verifier does. The open research is concentrated there, in verified state migration and token-based protocol invariants. The settled engineering covered by the recommendations above can be adopted almost directly.
