- ✅ **Phase 2 tasks 4 and 5 CLOSED without building, on measurement (2026-07-27).** **Task 4 (size-class freelist): do not build.** `bench/run_string_xlang.sh` compares `string_small_churn` against four baselines chosen to separate allocator overhead from representation — C++ (`std::string`, has SSO) 238ms, C (raw malloc) 400ms, Rust (`String`, no SSO — March's exact representation) 553ms, March 709ms, Python (pymalloc) 1249ms, all agreeing on the same checksum. Rust bounds what a freelist can win at ~1.3×, because a freelist attacks precisely the allocator and refcount overhead separating the two; C++ is ~3× and beats raw malloc, because you cannot out-allocate not allocating. The freelist buys roughly a third, forecloses the rest, and composes with nothing. **Task 5 (container-returning `split`): not needed as specified.** March has no flat array of heap pointers to build on (`Array` is a March-level persistent trie, `NativeArray` is unboxed int/float only), so it needs a whole new RC-aware container — and `String.index_of_from` already delivers 3.7× (975ms → 267ms counting 150K fields) with effectively zero allocation for scan-shaped callers. Reopen only with a workload that demonstrably needs every field retained.
