Headline
--------
(1) FLAT RSS: resident set is ~5 MB at EVERY contention level and stays DEAD flat
    for the whole run (see the *.rss traces — the 16-sibling, 31s run holds a
    constant 5012 KB start-to-finish; the sampler recorded no variation at all).
    Memory tracks the live set, not the allocation rate; ~10M transient
    boxed-Float allocations over a run are reclaimed by RC with no GC heap growth
    and no pause. (Absolute RSS is ~5 MB here vs ~2-3 MB on the M3 laptop — a
    page-size / allocator-arena difference between the two platforms; the
    property that matters, flatness across ops and contention, holds identically.)

(2) BOUNDED TAIL: p50/p90/p99 are FLAT at ~4us as CPU-bound siblings scale from 0
    to 4x the scheduler count — the scheduler never starves the common case, and
    p99 stays <= 8.2us across every contention level (tighter than the laptop's
    load-inflated p99, which reached 16.4us). The extreme p99.9 tail grows
    BOUNDED (16us -> 33us -> 4.2ms -> 8.4ms), not unbounded: there is no
    starvation. At 0 and 4 siblings — no / exactly-1x oversubscription of the 4
    sched threads — the idle-box p99.9 (16us, 33us) and max (513us, 4.9ms) are
    far TIGHTER than the load-contaminated laptop (65us, 262us; 3.4ms, 8.1ms),
    which is the whole point of re-running idle. At 8 and 16 siblings the idle
    p99.9 is LARGER than the laptop's, because this 4-core box is physically
    oversubscribed 2x / 4x whereas the 14-core laptop still had spare cores for
    the same sibling count — genuine CPU starvation on 4 cores, not contamination.

The hot request kernel is compiler-enforced zero-allocation via `cap no_alloc`
(injecting a tuple fails --check with "tuple construction allocates in a
`cap no_alloc` module").
